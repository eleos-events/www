## Style recommendations

### Colour palette

Your posters mix dark backgrounds with vibrant accent colours. To maintain brand coherence:

1. **Base** – rich blacks or very dark greys for backgrounds. This creates a cinematic feel and allows lighting effects (smoke, lasers) to stand out.
2. **Primary accent** – pick one signature neon colour (e.g., the green from the Groove Garden poster) for buttons, hover states and highlights. Neon colours convey energy and modernity and can be swapped to match each event theme (e.g., orange during Halloween). Avoid using too many colours simultaneously; keep the accent consistent across a season.
3. **Secondary accent** – use muted warm greys or off‑white for sections like the About page. Pastel gradients (inspired by Hï Ibiza’s backgrounds【704378123102882†screenshot】) can be introduced sparingly to separate sections or call attention to forms.

### Typography

- **Logo & headlines** – use your existing logo fonts: **Archivo Black** for the main brand name and **Aileron Black Italic** for “events”. These fonts are bold and commanding, ideal for headlines and hero text. Consider using **Archivo Bold** or **Aileron Bold** for secondary headings.
- **Body copy** – choose a clean, highly legible sans‑serif. Options include **Inter**, **Lato** or **Open Sans**; they pair well with Archivo and Aileron and render well on web. They also support various weights for hierarchy. Keep paragraphs short (3–4 sentences) to improve readability.
- **Numerals & labels** – consider using a monospace or condensed font for event times or ticket prices to differentiate them.

### Layout & interactions

- **Full‑width media** – replicate the immersive feel of UNVRS and Hï Ibiza by using full‑width images or videos at the top of sections. Use subtle parallax or fade‑in animations when scrolling to enhance depth without overwhelming the visitor.
- **Carousels & sliders** – for events, merchandise and news sections, implement horizontal sliders with arrow controls. Ensure cards are accessible and allow keyboard navigation.
- **Consistency in buttons** – adopt one button style: a filled button with the primary accent colour for main actions (e.g., “Get tickets”, “Join guest list”) and an outlined version for secondary actions. Rounded corners can complement the circular motifs in your logo.
- **Forms** – keep forms concise. Use floating labels or clear placeholders. Provide checkboxes for consent where required, as seen on UNVRS and Hï Ibiza mailing list forms【35321748743088†screenshot】【96315676228431†screenshot】.
- **Navigation** – use a sticky top bar with minimal items: Home, Events, About, Artists, Store, VIP, Blog, Contact. Include a hamburger menu for mobile. Keep the bar semi‑transparent over hero media.
- **Accessibility** – ensure sufficient colour contrast (WCAG 2.1 AA). Provide alt text for images and transcripts for videos. Allow keyboard navigation for sliders and forms.

### Imagery & content

- Use high‑resolution photographs and short video clips from your events. Shots of lighting rigs, crowds and performers help visitors feel the atmosphere. When possible, commission event photography that matches your dark‑and‑neon aesthetic.
- Integrate your existing posters as hero images on the event detail pages. For example, the Haunted House poster with burnt orange text can accompany its event page, while Groove Garden’s neon green theme can inform the colour palette for that event’s promotional page.
- Where content is text‑heavy (About page or news articles), break it up with quotes, lists or pull‑outs. Use icons to highlight points (e.g., location markers, genres).

### Technical considerations

- Optimise images and video for fast loading (lazy loading and responsive sizes).
- Implement SEO best practices: descriptive page titles, meta descriptions, structured data for events (e.g., schema.org `Event`), and alt text.
- Use an event management plugin or custom CMS features for easy creation of new event pages and news posts.
- Ensure mobile responsiveness – many visitors will view on phones. Test carousels and forms on smaller screens.

## Conclusion

The proposed website structure combines the immersive, high‑production aesthetic of leading nightlife venues with the bespoke, locally focused mission of elëos events. By using dark backgrounds, neon accents, bold typography and interactive elements such as carousels and sign‑up forms, the site will convey professionalism and energy while remaining user‑friendly and accessible.
